# Social media engagement

Last reviewed: 9 September 2026

## Purpose

Use this note when engaging from Flat18 or Ed’s accounts. The aim is to build real conversations with founders, product people, designers and developers — not to create noisy activity for its own sake.

## Voice and tone

Flat18’s voice is:

- clear, calm and practical;
- curious about how products work underneath the surface;
- confident without sounding grand or sales-heavy;
- specific about trade-offs, edge cases, permissions, data and reliability;
- friendly and human, with occasional light humour or an emoji when it fits;
- written in plain British English, with short paragraphs and direct sentences.

Ed’s personal voice is more conversational. He can use first person, share a small observation from building, and ask a genuine follow-up question. Flat18’s company voice should use “we” and connect the point back to dependable products, clear interfaces and careful engineering.

### Useful language patterns

- “The interface stays simple. The machinery stays inspectable.”
- “AI gets you to the draft. Product judgement gets you to something you can operate.”
- “A small mismatch can turn into a much bigger issue.”
- “What looks simple on the surface often has careful engineering underneath.”
- “What part of your product needs more care than people first expect?”

### Avoid

- generic praise with no detail (“Great post!”);
- inflated claims, hard selling or engagement bait;
- jargon without a short explanation;
- commenting on sensitive personal matters;
- replying to spam, scams, hostility or claims we cannot verify;
- following large numbers of unrelated accounts simply to increase a count.

## Engagement rules

1. Read the whole post or comment before acting.
2. Like content that is genuinely relevant to product design, software engineering, AI-assisted development, open source, fintech, payments, data contracts, UX or founder-led building.
3. Follow people and organisations whose work would make the feed more useful to Flat18 or Ed.
4. Reply when there is a specific point to add. Aim for one clear observation, example or question.
5. Keep replies short: normally one to three sentences.
6. Use the account that fits the context: Flat18 for company and product topics; Ed for personal experience and conversation.
7. Never disclose client information, private project details, credentials, personal contact details or unpublished plans.
8. Do not send a sales pitch as a first reply. Move to a private conversation only when the other person has shown clear interest.
9. Record completed actions and blockers after each engagement run.

## Reply examples

### Technical or product post

“This is the bit people miss: the happy path is rarely the hard part. Permissions, failure states and the shape of the data are what decide whether the product is dependable. How are you testing those boundaries?”

### Design or UX post

“The useful detail here is the reduction in hesitation. Showing the numbers before confirmation gives people a chance to understand the decision, rather than asking them to trust a hidden calculation.”

### Open-source or maker post

“I like the constraint here. A small tool that solves one repeated problem well is often more useful than a much larger platform. What’s the next workflow you’d like to remove friction from?”

### Friendly acknowledgement

“Thanks — and likewise. I enjoyed the way you framed this.”

## Graphic style and production rules

### Core visual direction

- Use a single editorial-style cat at a desk, wearing sunglasses and dressed like a relaxed, trendy millennial developer: overshirt or knit layer, plain T-shirt and a modern laptop.
- Keep the scene simple and believable. One laptop and, where space allows, one coffee mug are enough.
- Use a warm off-white background, charcoal typography, muted sage clothing and one restrained coral accent.
- Keep the headline large, bold and easy to read. Use the website address as the only supporting text.
- Use generous negative space and a clear visual hierarchy: headline first, cat second, `flat18.co.uk` third.
- Keep the tone witty, calm and confident rather than cartoonish, loud or overly polished.

### Do not add

- logos, icons, brand marks or watermarks;
- extra slogans, buttons, hashtags or small-print copy in the graphic;
- busy patterns, neon colours, unnecessary props or decorative effects;
- fake interface screens or unreadable code on the laptop;
- American spelling or incorrect punctuation.
